# Search

Search has a page of its own. The sidebar's Search row, under Home, opens it, and so do Cmd-F and Cmd-L from anywhere. Its field sits in the main view's room at the top, right of Back and Forward: a pill as tall as theirs and drawn the same way, up to 480 pt wide, with a magnifying glass at its start (the field's `inset` keeps the text clear of it). It gets the focus with its text selected. A press on it goes to the field, not to the window drag that the rest of the room does. The results' title scrolls away with them, and the tabs and headings pin under the field (see [UI](ui.md#the-list-header)). The field is built after the view so it's drawn over that pinned bar. A search replaces what the view borrows, so it can't go out in the middle of the frame. The debounce sends before anything in the main view is built (`search_tick`), so the results' first frame already keeps the room for Back and Forward that the empty page leaves out, and Return asks for its search once the frame is done (`Search_Now`). Without a query the page shows a magnifying glass and what can be searched for. While a search is out it says so, and after a failed one it shows why. Text left in the field from an earlier search is searched for again when the page opens, so its results come back, from memory if they're recent.

On the page, Cmd-F and Cmd-L select the field's text, and text typed after them in the same frame replaces it. Off the page the field isn't built, so the key opens the page and the field takes the focus when it's drawn in the next frame. Text typed in the same frame as that key is lost. A frame that leaves the page drops the field's shortcuts (`ui.drop_shortcuts`), so a Cmd-F in the next frame opens the page again instead of focusing a field that's gone.

Every edit raises the query generation and throws out queued Search jobs. Once typing has stopped for 250 ms (`SEARCH_DEBOUNCE`, a `ui.wake_at`, not a running timer), a `Search` job asks TIDAL's `search` for songs, albums, artists and playlists in one request, 50 of each. Nothing goes while the input method still has marked text, so composing にほんご doesn't search for each kana. Return searches at once, for the text it was pressed on: an edit after it in the same frame starts the debounce over, as it would in the next frame. A result whose generation isn't the latest is dropped, and the auth generation drops results from a login that has ended, as for every job. The app logs each query it sends and what came back.

The songs become the open track list, a `Track_View` with source `.Search`. So covers, double-click to play, the queue, the playing row and Enter work as in a playlist, and more songs load as `Tracks` jobs that page through `search` with only tracks, up to TIDAL's 300. The old results stay on screen until the new ones arrive. Tabs above the table switch to the albums, artists and playlists found, shown as rows. A click opens an album's tracks (`albums/{id}/tracks`) or a playlist, each as a track list of its own, or an artist's page, whose top tracks (`artists/{id}/toptracks`) are the open list (see [UI](ui.md#the-artist-page)). Picking a sidebar entry goes back to it. Clearing the field, by deleting the text or with Escape, empties the page and opens the sidebar's playlist again under it if the results were showing.

Opening any list, from the sidebar, the tabs or the track menu, ends a search that's still waiting for its debounce or its answer. The generation goes up and the queued Search job goes, so the answer can't bring the results back over the list. The text stays in the field, and the search page searches for it again.

The search page is a place in the history, `Search_Place` with no query. A search becomes a place when its results show, not when it's sent. Searches in a row are one place, so the results replace the empty page, and clearing the field puts the empty page back in their place. A search that fails leaves the history at the search page. Back or Forward to a search moves the history there once the results come, and leaves it where it was if they don't. Either way, the next Back or Forward steps on from that search, so pressing Back again walks past a search that fails or hasn't answered.

Signing out clears the field, the results and the history Back and Forward go through, so the next account starts with none of them.

An album result's artists are links to their pages. The track menu's Go to album and Go to artist open the same lists, from the row's album and artist ids. A song by several artists gets a Go to item for each of them in place of Go to artist. The menu offers these only when the row has the ids. Copy song link puts `https://tidal.com/browse/track/<id>` on the clipboard. The menu belongs to the list and the track it was opened on: when another list replaces that list (a search result, a sidebar entry, an album), or the row holds another track, the menu closes and its actions do nothing. Play next and Add to queue put the track in the play queue, after the current track or at the end of the play order (see [Playback](playback.md#queue-and-gapless)).

With `TIDALFAST_FAKE=1` the search runs over the 10,000 fake rows on the UI thread. It matches title, artist or album, ignoring case, and its albums and artists are the distinct albums and artists of the matching songs, up to 50 of each. "Jóga" matches 145 songs by title, from 26 albums and 34 artists. Going to an album lists the fake rows with that name. Going to an artist opens its page, made from the fake rows: its rows are its top tracks, their albums its albums, EPs and singles, and the artists of the other rows on those albums the similar artists.
